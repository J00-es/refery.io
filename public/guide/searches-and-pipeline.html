What the words mean

  • Proposed to you. We think your bench fits. One tap says yes or no; an unanswered proposal lapses after 7 days and can be re-proposed later.
  • Working. You are on it. The client name, the full brief and Submit are open.
  • Open to you, on request. Real seats, real facts (title, city, band, stage), but the client is described rather than named until you are on a search there.
  • The stage word (Sourcing, Shortlisting, Client interviewing, Offer out, Filled) tells you how far the search has got. It never shows how many other partners are on it.
  • "2 of yours in play" counts only your own submissions.

Stages, in order

SourcingShortlistingClient interviewingOffer outFilled

Sourcing: nobody is in front of the client yet, send as soon as you have conviction. Offer out: send only someone exceptional now. Filled: closed; anyone you submitted stays yours.

The bar for this seat

Hard requirements, from the JD

Not for: people who need a spec handed to them, or whose last three roles were at 1,000-person companies.

Logistics

Onsite five days · US work authorisation required, no new sponsorship · start within 6 weeks preferred

Two questions to ask before you submit

  1. "Walk me through something you shipped end to end without a spec."
  2. "Are you in the Bay Area now, and are you authorised to work in the US without sponsorship?"

How they interview

1 Intro call with a co-founder · 2 Technical stage, live coding or a take-home · 3 On-site with the team. Decision inside 10 days of the first call.

Questions 2

Would they consider someone on an H-1B transfer?
Yes for a transfer, no for new sponsorship. Answered by Refery, 2 days ago.

Reading the page

  • Posted as. The public title differs from the real seat sometimes. Use the headline when you talk to candidates.
  • Hard requirements come from the job description. From the intake call is what the founder said out loud, including what will not clear. That second list is the one that matters.
  • Two questions to ask. If a candidate cannot answer both well, do not submit them yet.
  • Ask a question. Anything unclear, ask here rather than by email. The answer is published to everyone on the search, so one question helps all of you.
  • Client brief opens the longer document for the whole client: the company, the team, the story, how they decide.
  • Headcount and slots. "Open-ended" means submit as many as clear the bar. A capped seat shows how many slots are left.

The one rule of thumb: if you would not put your own name behind the person in a call with the founder, do not submit them. A small number of strong submissions is worth more to you here than volume.

What is required, and why

  • Your own candidate. Someone in your Candidates book. Submitting from someone else's book would take credit for their sourcing.
  • A CV and a way to reach them on the profile.
  • How you know them. One honest sentence. This is what makes a marketplace worth reading.
  • You can introduce them now. Submitting says so. If we ask for the intro and it never comes, we will ask you before anything else happens.
  • Work authorisation is the first thing every client asks. If you leave it blank we will ask you for it.
  • Why they fit is optional but it is the difference between a read and a skim.

Things the form protects you from

  • A person another partner already submitted to this seat shows as taken, without a name, so you do not waste an evening.
  • A closed or full seat will not accept a submission.
  • Your answers on comp and authorisation are remembered on the candidate, so the next submission starts full.

  • Intro asked. The profile is strong for a live seat. You get an email asking for a warm introduction, naming the seat.   • In the pool. Strong profile, nothing live for them today. We keep them under your name and you hear first the moment a search opens that fits; we ask you for the intro then.

After the call

Once Lily has spoken to them you get a short update: the honest read, and what happens next (shared with founders, kept warm for the right seat, or not a fit after the call). Nothing about your candidate goes to a client before that call.

Three doors

  • Add candidate. One PDF. We read it end to end, show you what we captured, ask the three questions, then create the profile.
  • Bulk upload. Many PDFs at once. After they are read you get one table to fill the gaps, with "same for all" shortcuts.
  • Email. Send a CV as a PDF attachment to lily@refery.io from the address you signed up with. It becomes a profile under your name automatically.

Why the three questions

  • Every live seat today needs US work authorisation. Without that answer, no seat can be evaluated. With it, most can.
  • Most seats are onsite in SF or New York. Cities and relocation decide half the matches.
  • The base band saves a round trip with the founder later.
  • Skip anything you do not know. The profile shows what is missing and you can answer any time; we will also ask you in the first email if it matters for a seat.

The rules that protect you

Short version. The full terms are in your agreement.

Your candidates stay yours.Once you have submitted someone, a second partner sending the same person sees "already submitted" and nothing more. The terms of your agreement cover the rest.
Introduce when asked.Submitting says you can introduce them now. If we ask and it does not happen, we check with you before anything else. "Send the intro email for me" is always there.
Tell your candidates.Say you are sharing their profile with Refery before you upload it. The upload asks; answer honestly.
Client names stay inside the desk.Before you are on a search the client is described, not named. Once you are on it, the name and brief are for you and your candidate, not for public postings; abridged, unnamed versions are fine.
Withdraw, do not ghost.If a person takes another job, withdraw them from the search. It keeps the client's read of you clean.
Ask in the search.Questions asked on the search page are answered once and published to everyone on it, which is faster for you and fairer for all.

Your beta week

Five things to try, and where to tell us what broke or what was confusing.

Try these

  1. Open Searches. Answer any proposal. Ask to be put on one search that fits your bench, with one line on why.
  2. Open one search you are on. Read the bar and the two questions. Ask a question if anything is unclear.
  3. Add two people from your book, or email us two CVs. Answer the three questions on at least one.
  4. Submit one person to one search, with "why they fit" and "how you know them" filled in.
  5. Open Pipeline the next day. Check the status and the timeline on the person's profile. On Sunday, check the recap against what you saw.

Tell us

  • Anything that was unclear, wrong, or slower than it should be. A screenshot and one line is enough.
  • Reply to any Refery email, or write to lily@refery.io. If you are in our Slack, use the channel you were invited to.
  • What you would want on Sunday that is not there yet.

Thank you. You are the first partners on the desk. What you tell us this week is what the next version is built from.
